TITLE: # The Slow Fruit of the Kingdom

# The Slow Fruit of the Kingdom
There is a kind of fruit I keep wanting God to produce quickly\. I want faithfulness to show visible results\. I want obedience to lead somewhere I can explain\. Yet Jesus speaks of the Kingdom differently\. He says, “I am the vine; you are the branches\. Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit” \(John 15:5\)\. Fruit, in His telling, comes from abiding, not urgency, and from remaining, not managing outcomes\.

This unsettles me because I have learned to measure life by what can be seen\. Progress\. Outcomes\. Evidence that something is working\. Even spiritually, I am tempted to translate fruitfulness into productivity\. But the Kingdom operates differently\. Fruit does not come from striving, but from remaining in Christ\. It grows through connection, not control\.

The fruit of the Spirit matures quietly\. Love that deepens without applause\. Peace that steadies the soul while questions remain unanswered\. Faithfulness that continues without clarity or affirmation\. None of this can be rushed\. Much of it forms in ordinary days where obedience feels repetitive and nothing looks impressive\.

I am learning that anxiety often comes from trying to harvest what God is still growing\. From digging into the soil too early to check progress\. The Father is not a manager counting outcomes\. He is a gardener who understands seasons\. He knows when to prune and when to wait\.

The fruit of the Kingdom is slow, but it is real\. And when it comes, it lasts\. Remaining in Christ is not inactivity\. It is trust that God is at work beneath the surface, forming something deeper than I could ever measure\.
