# Responsive Coaching Journey Process

Purpose: Define a practical process for entering the faith journey with another person through responsive coaching.

Alignment requirement:
- This process must align with:
  - [docs/governance/theological-convictions.md](docs/governance/theological-convictions.md)
  - [docs/governance/critical-faith-markers.md](docs/governance/critical-faith-markers.md)
  - [docs/governance/tangible-activities-from-convictions.md](docs/governance/tangible-activities-from-convictions.md)

## Core Process Principles

- The process is non-linear and dynamic for every person.
- Progress happens over time through trust, not pressure.
- The foundation is love for the person as Jesus taught, not transactional outcomes.
- Discernment is continuous: coaching responds to what God is already doing.
- Pace is relational: depth follows trust, clarity, and readiness.

## Starting Input Area (Add Your Points Here)

Use this area for additional process insights or field notes.

1. Start in conversation: listen and truly see the person.
2. Assess spiritual openness with gentle questions.
3. Explore where the person is in their faith journey.
4. Discern where God is already at work by the Holy Spirit.
5. Align the next coaching step to that discerned movement.

## Draft Journey Flow (Version 0)

1. Presence and posture (entry)
- Enter with humility, curiosity, and non-manipulative intent.
- Objective: establish trust and relational safety.
- Stage checkpoint: person feels seen, not managed.

2. First contact and listening
- Ask simple, human questions and listen for story, longing, pain, hope.
- Objective: understand the person before offering interpretation.
- Stage checkpoint: person shares meaningful life context willingly.

3. Discernment in real time
- Notice where God may already be at work in the person's life.
- Objective: respond to what is present, not force a scripted track.
- Stage checkpoint: one clear discerned thread is identified.

4. Gentle naming and reflection
- Reflect back signs of meaning, goodness, dependence, and spiritual openness.
- Objective: help the person recognize lived faith signals in their own story.
- Stage checkpoint: person acknowledges at least one recognized faith signal.

5. Kingdom framing in common ground
- Connect lived experience to Jesus' teaching of the Kingdom in shared areas.
- Objective: move from abstract spirituality toward coherent Kingdom language.
- Stage checkpoint: person can articulate common ground in their own words.

6. Personalization of faith language
- Encourage shift from conceptual language to personal relational language.
- Objective: deepen articulation of how God engages them personally.
- Stage checkpoint: language shifts from generic deity terms toward personal engagement.

7. Invitation to surrender and practice
- Invite concrete next-step practices: prayer, submission, reconciliation, service.
- Objective: move from insight to trust-based response.
- Stage checkpoint: person names and commits to one lived next step.

8. Community connection
- Introduce trusted people and communities that reinforce authentic movement.
- Objective: prevent isolation and support sustained growth.
- Stage checkpoint: person has at least one trusted relational/community connection.

9. Ongoing coaching rhythm
- Continue check-ins, reflection loops, and adaptive next steps.
- Objective: normalize faith as daily life and strengthen resilience.
- Stage checkpoint: regular rhythm established and maintained.

10. Journey shift recognition
- Identify meaningful shifts with marker evidence and testimony.
- Objective: document growth, celebrate progress, and clarify next formation goals.
- Stage checkpoint: shift markers recorded with supporting evidence.

## Entry Process Quickstart (First 2-3 Sessions)

Session 1: trust and story
- Focus: stages 1-2
- Output: relational baseline and core life context

Session 2: discernment and naming
- Focus: stages 3-4
- Output: discerned thread plus initial faith-signal reflection

Session 3: framing and next step
- Focus: stages 5-7
- Output: one clear next practice and follow-up plan

Note: Move to stage 8 or return to earlier stages based on trust, readiness, and discernment.

## Stage Entry and Exit Criteria (v1)

Use these criteria to determine whether a person is ready to enter a stage and whether the stage objective has been reached.

### Stage 1: Presence and posture

- Entry criteria:
  - First relational contact initiated.
  - Coach has confirmed non-manipulative intent.
- Exit criteria:
  - Person reports feeling respected and safe.
  - At least one follow-up touchpoint is agreed.

### Stage 2: First contact and listening

- Entry criteria:
  - Stage 1 exit criteria met.
  - Person is willing to share life context.
- Exit criteria:
  - At least one meaningful narrative element captured (longing, pain, hope, resistance).
  - Coach records summary in session notes.

### Stage 3: Discernment in real time

- Entry criteria:
  - Stage 2 exit criteria met.
  - Person has provided enough context for discernment.
- Exit criteria:
  - One discerned spiritual thread identified and documented.
  - At least one aligned marker (M/J) mapped.

### Stage 4: Gentle naming and reflection

- Entry criteria:
  - Stage 3 exit criteria met.
  - Person is open to reflective feedback.
- Exit criteria:
  - Coach reflects one clear faith signal back to person.
  - Person acknowledges, clarifies, or meaningfully engages that signal.

### Stage 5: Kingdom framing in common ground

- Entry criteria:
  - Stage 4 exit criteria met.
  - Shared language space is present for Kingdom framing.
- Exit criteria:
  - Person can restate one Kingdom connection in their own words.
  - At least one activity anchor (A) linked to that connection.

### Stage 6: Personalization of faith language

- Entry criteria:
  - Stage 5 exit criteria met.
  - Person demonstrates openness to personal framing.
- Exit criteria:
  - Observable movement from generic to personal faith language.
  - Marker alignment includes M9 and/or M11 when applicable.

### Stage 7: Invitation to surrender and practice

- Entry criteria:
  - Stage 6 exit criteria met.
  - Person is ready to discuss concrete next practice.
- Exit criteria:
  - One next-step practice is named and committed.
  - Follow-up checkpoint date is recorded.

### Stage 8: Community connection

- Entry criteria:
  - Stage 7 exit criteria met or equivalent readiness observed.
  - Person is open to trusted relational support.
- Exit criteria:
  - At least one trusted person or community connection is identified.
  - Connection step is completed or scheduled.

### Stage 9: Ongoing coaching rhythm

- Entry criteria:
  - Stage 8 exit criteria met.
  - Two or more coaching touchpoints completed.
- Exit criteria:
  - Recurring coaching rhythm is established (for example monthly check-in).
  - At least one adaptive loop documented from prior session learning.

### Stage 10: Journey shift recognition

- Entry criteria:
  - Stage 9 rhythm established.
  - Sufficient evidence exists to evaluate meaningful shift.
- Exit criteria:
  - At least one journey shift marker (J1-J6) confirmed with supporting evidence.
  - Growth summary and next formation goal documented.

Operational rule:

- A person may move backward to an earlier stage when trust, readiness, or context requires it.
- Backward movement is not failure; it is responsive coaching fidelity.

## Monthly Quantitative Stage Metrics (v1)

These metrics quantify journey-process flow without replacing qualitative discernment.

### JP1. Active participants by stage

- Definition: number of distinct participants with activity in each stage during month.
- Formula: distinct participant count by stage and month.

### JP2. Stage entry count

- Definition: number of participants entering each stage during month.
- Formula: count of stage-entry events by stage and month.

### JP3. Stage completion count

- Definition: number of participants meeting exit criteria for each stage during month.
- Formula: count of stage-exit events by stage and month.

### JP4. Stage completion rate

- Definition: share of participants who complete a stage after entering it in the same month.
- Formula: stage completion count / stage entry count.

### JP5. Forward progression rate

- Definition: share of participants who move to the next stage within 30 days.
- Formula: participants advancing to next stage within 30 days / participants who entered stage.

### JP6. Re-entry rate

- Definition: share of participants who return to an earlier stage after advancing.
- Formula: participants with backward stage movement / participants active in month.

### JP7. Median days in stage

- Definition: median elapsed days between stage entry and stage exit.
- Formula: median(stage exit date - stage entry date) for completed stage rows.

### JP8. Follow-up reliability

- Definition: share of committed follow-up checkpoints completed on time.
- Formula: on-time follow-ups / total scheduled follow-ups.

### JP9. Journey shift capture rate

- Definition: share of active participants with at least one journey shift marker confirmed in month.
- Formula: participants with confirmed J1-J6 in month / active participants in month.

### JP10. Evidence completeness rate

- Definition: share of stage events with required anchor mapping and notes.
- Required fields: stage, participant, date, marker IDs, activity IDs, evidence note.
- Formula: complete stage-event rows / total stage-event rows.

## A1 Visibility in Monthly Metrics

A1 definition:

- A1 = Relational first-contact.

Where A1 should appear in stage flow:

- Primarily Stage 1 (Presence and posture) and Stage 2 (First contact and listening).
- Optionally later stages when a new relational first-contact occurs with a different person/context.

How A1 is counted monthly:

- A1 Event Count: number of stage events where `activity_ids` includes `A1`.
- A1 Unique Participants: distinct participants with at least one A1-tagged event in month.
- A1 Completion Yield: A1-tagged participants who reach Stage 2 exit criteria within 30 days / A1-tagged participants.

How A1 connects to JP metrics:

- JP2 (Stage entry count): A1 should be visible in Stage 1 and Stage 2 entry volume.
- JP3 (Stage completion count): A1-linked records should contribute to early-stage completion visibility.
- JP5 (Forward progression rate): A1 Completion Yield is a focused subset of JP5 for first-contact fidelity.
- JP10 (Evidence completeness): A1 rows are incomplete unless `activity_ids` explicitly includes `A1` when applicable.

Minimum reporting line for A1 each month:

- `A1 events | A1 unique participants | A1 completion yield | notes on barriers`

Monthly review thresholds (initial):

- JP4 Stage completion rate: target 60%+
- JP5 Forward progression rate: target 50%+
- JP8 Follow-up reliability: target 80%+
- JP10 Evidence completeness: target 95%+

Calibration note:

- Thresholds are initial operating targets and should be recalibrated after one full quarter of real data.
